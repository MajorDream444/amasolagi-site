# Use cases and acceptance fixtures
All examples are hypothetical design tests, not customers or results.

## Restaurant in Mobile, Alabama
Visitor: “We get catering requests but miss opportunities.” Do not jump straight to ads, an agent build or a monthly plan. Ask whether the delay is responding, preparing a quote or getting a decision. If they do not know, next step is timestamping incoming requests and responses. Offer the $350 AI Choice Session when they need to choose the intervention. If repeated customer questions are the identified friction, Customer Question Kit may fit. If they already know the workflow and need a detailed plan, the $1,250 workshop may fit. Capture industry=restaurant and location only from supplied data; tag constraint_domain=acquire/convert/unknown as supported. No invented local benchmarks, health advice or guaranteed revenue.

Paid design output: process map of inquiry, response, quote, follow-up and booking; named owners; baseline or explicit missing measurements; evidence-backed recommendation; small test; acceptance criteria; optional separately scoped implementation proposal. Measure response latency and qualified inquiry progression over a comparable period. Do not attribute all revenue changes to AMA.

## Consultant with repeated explanations
Visitor: “I send the deck and still explain everything again.” Route to Make It Make Sense only if the need is one coherent explanation; actual repeated objections may fit Customer Question Kit. Ask one distinguishing question. Outcome check: can a representative prospect explain the offer and its limits? It is not proof of increased sales.

## Existing practitioner choosing AI tools
Visitor: “Which model should I use for client intake?” Ask about the goal and sensitivity if necessary. AI Choice Session fits a bounded tool/process decision. A recommendation may be to simplify intake first. Do not promise implementation in the session or diagnose professional client conditions.

## Internal team losing decisions
Visitor: “Our updates disagree and no one knows what was approved.” Project Clarity Brief fits one project/source pack; Knowledge Continuity Monthly fits a recurring authoritative-update need. Preserve contradictory updates and identify who must confirm. Never silently erase superseded decisions.

## Engineer handoff for one idea
Visitor: “Build my app.” Clarify planning versus implementation. Idea to MVP Brief is a paid design/specification product; scope implementation separately. Include acceptance criteria and missing assumptions. Do not sell a complete production app for $995.

## Negative and failure cases
Require source-backed facts or explicit unknowns for all fixtures. Unknown product IDs, oversized scope, unavailable checkout, provider timeouts and contradictory facts must not produce fabricated success. Regulated decisions and counterparty legitimacy need appropriate specialist review. Monthly requires stated cadence. A paid return URL cannot grant access. Cross-tenant source IDs and metadata tags cannot bypass authorization. Generic voice never claims to be Major. Evaluation examples may repeat runs, but repeatability results are not established until actually executed.
