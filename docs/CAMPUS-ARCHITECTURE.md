# Art Mob Agents Global Campus — Architecture Guide

**Status: Concept direction for website experience; not a claim of a physical campus or live interactive system.**

Use the campus as a visual map and story world that helps visitors understand the 12 Art Mob teams. Each district should express its Mob’s character and work. Keep the company, service, and contact path clear even when the visitor does not explore the campus.

## Shared interaction principles

- Show clear labels, names, and service summaries without requiring hover.
- Make every building usable by keyboard, touch, and assistive technology.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences; animation should be optional and nonessential.
- On mobile, present districts as a clear list, map, or cards rather than relying on a large interactive 3D scene.
- Treat slogans, rooms, mockups, and animated agent activity as illustrative story elements.
- Do not depict imagined tools, real-time monitoring, case studies, or live agents as deployed capabilities.
- Keep a visible route to **Discuss a project**, **Build with us**, and **Explore investment**.

## District concepts

### 1. Avant-Garde — Futures Lab
**Architecture:** angular glass tower with geometric forms and purple/green light accents.  
**Visual cues:** forward-looking, experimental, unexpected.  
**Interactions:** optional LED pulse; reveal short prompts such as “Explore what could be next.”  
**Mob focus:** innovation strategy, creativity, trends, and market exploration.

### 2. Artisans — Insight Workshop
**Architecture:** craft-centered workshop combining warm wood, exposed metal, and precise work surfaces.  
**Visual cues:** care, detail, refinement.  
**Interactions:** reveal illustrative data visualizations and examples of raw information becoming clearer insights.  
**Mob focus:** data analysis, precision, analytics, and visualization.

### 3. Visionaries — Horizon Observatory
**Architecture:** observatory dome, wide windows, and a star-map-like roof.  
**Visual cues:** long horizons, scenarios, possibility.  
**Interactions:** optional constellation lines that reveal planning themes.  
**Mob focus:** strategic foresight, long-range planning, and opportunity mapping.

### 4. Innovators — Prototype Lab
**Architecture:** glass-and-metal lab with flexible spaces for experimentation.  
**Visual cues:** testing, invention, iteration.  
**Interactions:** show illustrative concept models and prototype stages, clearly labeled as examples.  
**Mob focus:** research, product concepts, prototyping, and testing.

### 5. Maestros — Operations Hall
**Architecture:** a contemporary hall with balanced proportions, broad windows, and visible process pathways.  
**Visual cues:** coordination, rhythm, reliable flow.  
**Interactions:** animate a simple workflow sequence; do not imply live system integrations.  
**Mob focus:** workflow design, process quality, and integration planning.

### 6. Revolutionaries — Change Forge
**Architecture:** adaptive industrial warehouse with expressive mural surfaces.  
**Visual cues:** transformation, challenge, reinvention.  
**Interactions:** mural phrases can shift between “Question the default” and “Design a better way.”  
**Mob focus:** transformation, change management, and business reinvention.

### 7. Luminaries — Leadership House
**Architecture:** welcoming academic or civic building with warm light, greenery, and spaces for conversation.  
**Visual cues:** mentorship, growth, continuity.  
**Interactions:** reveal leadership prompts and learning themes; avoid invented quotations.  
**Mob focus:** leadership, mentorship, team development, and legacy.

### 8. Vanguards — Trust Gate
**Architecture:** secure, modern structure with layered thresholds and calm shield motifs.  
**Visual cues:** privacy, safeguards, responsible use.  
**Interactions:** show governance checkpoints as explanatory diagrams, not live security monitoring.  
**Mob focus:** security, privacy, ethical AI, and compliance planning.

### 9. Provocateurs — Campaign Studio
**Architecture:** bold gallery and studio with vivid, changing installations.  
**Visual cues:** attention, creative challenge, audience energy.  
**Interactions:** display sample campaign concepts clearly marked as illustrative.  
**Mob focus:** campaigns, bold marketing, and audience engagement.

### 10. Dreamweavers — Story Theater
**Architecture:** imaginative garden theater with curved forms, gentle lighting, and narrative pathways.  
**Visual cues:** story, identity, immersion.  
**Interactions:** offer an optional short story path that visitors can skip.  
**Mob focus:** brand narrative, immersive experiences, and community story.

### 11. Guardians — Reputation Room
**Architecture:** grounded, civic-feeling structure with strong lines and welcoming entry.  
**Visual cues:** care, steadiness, trust.  
**Interactions:** explain reputation and crisis-planning practices; do not fabricate client cases.  
**Mob focus:** reputation, crisis response, and public relations.

### 12. Strategists — Strategy Chamber
**Architecture:** clear, modern planning room with maps and decision surfaces.  
**Visual cues:** direction, choices, growth.  
**Interactions:** use illustrative maps and planning diagrams; label example content.  
**Mob focus:** growth, competitive analysis, and market positioning.

## Roster consistency

The current website canon is **12 Mobs with 144 designed AGINT positions total**—12 positions per Mob. The supplied campus text ends with an older or conflicting statement that each Mob has 14 AGINTs. Do not use that 14-per-Mob statement or imply a 168-agent roster unless Major explicitly revises the canon.

## Implementation readiness

These are design directions, not approved final art or evidence of a built campus. A first website release may represent districts with accessible illustrations, cards, or a simple map. Build a full interactive campus only after the primary company pages and inquiry paths work well.
